# Using the portfolio to get work

A portfolio gets work when the right person lands on it, understands in ten seconds what you do, and trusts it. These steps are in rough order of payoff.

## 1. Pick a sharp pitch

"Android developer" is a crowded line. Your resume supports a rarer one: **Android apps that connect to hardware and stream in real time** (BLE, Wi-Fi Direct, WebRTC). Use it in the hero line, the LinkedIn headline and every proposal. Companies building devices, wearables, telehealth or live video have few engineers who have done this at scale.

## 2. Turn your services into clear offers

The "Work with me" section lists five. Make two of them concrete and priced, because fixed offers are easier to say yes to:

- **Android crash and performance audit.** Fixed price, one week, a written report with fixes ranked by impact. Your 12% to 4% crash-rate result is the proof.
- **Compose migration sprint.** Move a set of screens to Jetpack Compose with tests.
- **BLE or Wi-Fi Direct proof of concept.** A working pairing flow in two weeks.

## 3. Show proof you are allowed to show

Your Meta and Sony work is under client confidentiality, so the stories on the site stay at the level your resume already uses. Add public proof next to them:

- Two or three small, clean-room demo apps on GitHub (a Compose and Room offline app, a WebRTC sample, a BLE scanner). Fill the `projects` list so the Projects section appears.
- A 60 to 90 second screen recording of each.
- One short article per demo on how you built it.
- Real testimonials from past managers or clients, with permission, in the `testimonials` list.

## 4. Where to look for each kind of work

Availability and fees change, so check each site's current terms.

| Goal | Places to start |
| --- | --- |
| Freelance projects | Upwork, Contra, Fiverr Pro, direct outreach |
| Vetted contract marketplaces | Toptal, Arc, Lemon.io, Gun.io, Braintrust |
| Startup full-time and part-time | Wellfound, LinkedIn Jobs, Cutshort, Instahyre |
| India-based roles | Naukri, LinkedIn, Instahyre, referrals from past colleagues |
| Part-time and retainers | Your Meta and Sony contacts, Android and Kotlin communities, meetups in Bengaluru |

## 5. Direct outreach beats waiting

Each week, send ten short, specific messages to founders and engineering leads at companies building hardware, streaming or health apps. Name one thing you noticed about their app and one way you would help. Link the portfolio. A short message with a relevant example outperforms a long generic one.

## 6. Put the link everywhere

LinkedIn (Featured section and headline), GitHub profile README, resume header, email signature, Upwork profile, and the end of every proposal.

## 7. Part-time and freelance basics

- State your hours per week, your time zone (IST, UTC+5:30) and the overlap hours you can offer to Europe, the Middle East or Australia.
- Quote a fixed price for a fixed scope, or a monthly retainer for ongoing work. Avoid open-ended hourly work with no cap.
- Use a written agreement and take a deposit. Keep your employer's contract in mind: check any moonlighting or IP clause before taking side work.
- Add a "Book a call" link (`profile.bookingUrl`) so interested people can book in one click.

## 8. Keep the availability pill honest

Set `profile.availability.text` to what is true this month, for example "Booking from December" or "One part-time slot open". Update it whenever it changes.
